Fix subtitle delay without making sync worse

A fixed offset moves every cue by the same amount. It works when the subtitles and video have the same edit but start at different times. It cannot repair missing scenes or a gradually increasing timing error.

Measure before you move

Pause at a clearly audible word near the beginning. Record when the word is spoken and when its subtitle starts. Repeat near the middle and end. Calculate offset = desired start − current subtitle start, using seconds.

Spoken word:     00:00:04.000
Subtitle start:  00:00:06.000
Offset:         4 − 6 = −2 seconds

In this example, enter −2 in the workspace. A cue from 6 to 8.5 seconds becomes a cue from 4 to 6.5 seconds. Its duration remains 2.5 seconds, and the caption text stays the same.

Recognize the three common patterns

  • Constant delay: the captions are about two seconds late throughout. One offset is appropriate.
  • Progressive drift: the error is one second at the beginning and ten seconds near the end. A fixed offset will only align one part. Use an editor with time scaling or frame-rate correction after confirming the cause.
  • A sudden jump: the timing changes after a cut, intro or advertisement. Find a subtitle file for the same video edit or adjust separate sections in an editor.

Why a negative offset can fail

If your first cue starts at 1 second, an offset of −2 would place it before zero. Subtitle Studio rejects the whole operation rather than silently clipping or deleting that cue. Reduce the offset or fix the early cue in a subtitle editor. Do not simply change the sign: that shifts every cue in the opposite direction.

A repeatable check

  1. Keep your original file and download the sample below.
  2. Load the sample, enter −2, and process as SRT.
  3. Compare the output with the expected SRT file.
  4. For your own file, preview the beginning, middle and end against the video before publishing.
